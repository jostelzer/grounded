# Question
What can a person with stage 2 hypertension do to lower blood pressure without medication, and how much does each thing actually lower it?

# Angles
A1 lifestyle-overview — umbrella/systematic syntheses of non-pharmacological BP lowering (which interventions, effect sizes in mmHg)
A2 diet-sodium — sodium reduction, DASH/dietary patterns, potassium substitution
A3 exercise — aerobic, dynamic resistance, isometric training; network meta-analyses of modality
A4 weight-alcohol — weight loss, alcohol reduction, and other behavioural levers (sleep, stress/breathing)
A5 limits-contrary — null/negative trials, adherence and durability, severity ceiling (is lifestyle enough at stage 2?), harms/cautions

# Reading notes (load-bearing full texts and cite-likely abstracts)

## Full texts read
- `Huang2020effect` — Design: systematic review and meta-analysis of 133 randomised
  trials, 12,197 adults, sodium intake measured by 24-hour urinary excretion.
  Result: a mean 130 mmol/day reduction lowered systolic BP by 4.26 mmHg
  (3.62–4.89) and diastolic by 2.07 (1.67–2.48); each 50 mmol cut 1.10 mmHg
  systolic (0.66–1.54); falls were larger in older people, non-white populations
  and those with higher baseline systolic BP, and trials under 15 days
  underestimated the effect (1.05 vs 2.13 mmHg per 50 mmol). Limitation: the
  dose-response only appears in multivariable meta-regression — unadjusted
  analyses showed no association with the size of the sodium cut. Synthesis use: supports the sodium dose number and the inference that a
  higher starting blood pressure means a bigger payoff.
- `Chen2024updated` — Design: network meta-analysis of 54 randomised trials,
  5,827 patients with primary hypertension, 21 non-drug interventions.
  Result: against usual care, aerobic exercise cut systolic BP 5.45 mmHg
  (1.86–9.04), DASH 4.47 (1.25–7.68) and tai chi 8.72 (4.70–12.73), while the
  top-ranked combinations reached 21.75 mmHg (10.02–33.25) for lifestyle plus
  tai chi and 17.00 (7.72–26.28) for breathing exercise. Limitation: CINeMA
  confidence ranged from very low to moderate across most comparisons, and the
  combination estimates rest on few, small, wide-interval trials. Synthesis use: supports the claim that stacking levers beats any single one,
  with the certainty of the evidence attached as context.
- `Edwards2024isometric` — Design: expert-informed narrative review of isometric
  exercise training for blood pressure, summarising the meta-analytic record and
  grading certainty by author-panel GRADE. Result: meta-analyses pool systolic
  and diastolic reductions of 5–9 and 1–4 mmHg, with indirect subgroup estimates
  of 11.41/5.09 mmHg for the wall squat, 9.96/3.69 for leg extension and
  8.34/4.09 for handgrip. Limitation: trial quality is mostly mid-range on
  TESTEX with unblinded assessors and weak placebo control, head-to-head
  comparisons against aerobic training conflict, some studies show blood
  pressure returning toward baseline within about ten days of stopping, and
  safety evidence covers healthy, pre-hypertensive and stage 1 populations
  rather than complex ones. Synthesis use: the isometric promise plus its honest
  discount and the detraining catch.

## Cite-likely (abstract level)
- `Carey2022treatment` — JAMA review. Lifestyle modification is first-line therapy (weight loss, low-sodium/high-potassium dietary pattern, physical activity, alcohol moderation); component effects are partially additive and enhance drug efficacy; decision to start medication rests on BP level and ASCVD risk; a 10 mmHg SBP reduction cuts CVD events ~20–30%. Use: framing, the additivity claim, and why mmHg matter.
- `Edwards2023exercise` — pairwise + network MA, 270 RCTs, 15,827 participants. Aerobic −4.49/−2.53, dynamic resistance −4.55/−3.04, combined −6.04/−2.54, HIIT −4.08/−2.50, isometric −8.24/−4.00 mmHg. SUCRA ranks isometric first (98.3%). Use: the per-modality numbers.
- `Naci2018does` — NMA of 391 RCTs (197 exercise, 10,461 participants; 194 drug, 29,281). No direct exercise-vs-drug trials; only 56 exercise trials enrolled hypertensive participants (3,508 people). Use: the honest limit on the exercise-vs-drug comparison.
- `Hansford2021effectiveness` — SR/MA, 24 trials, n=1,143, adults with BP ≥130/85. Office SBP −6.97 (−8.77 to −5.18), office DBP −3.86 (−5.31 to −2.41); 24-h ambulatory SBP not significant (−2.77, −6.80 to 1.25); certainty very low/low, high heterogeneity; no significant excess risk (RR 1.12, 0.47 to …). Use: the office-vs-ambulatory discount on isometric training.
- `Sacks2001effects` — DASH-Sodium feeding trial, 412 participants. Sodium high→intermediate −2.1 mmHg SBP on control diet, −1.3 on DASH; intermediate→low a further −4.6 and −1.7. DASH lowered SBP at every sodium level; effects in hypertensive and non-hypertensive people. Use: diet and salt are partly redundant, and both matter.
- `Schwingshackl2018comparative` — NMA, 67 trials, 13 dietary approaches, 17,230 hypertensive/pre-hypertensive participants; DASH, Mediterranean, low-carbohydrate, Palaeolithic, high-protein, low-GI, low-sodium and low-fat all beat control for SBP (range up to −8.73 mmHg). Use: no single magic diet; several patterns work.
- `Juraschek2026dash` — GoFresh RCT, Black adults in Boston with SBP 120–<150 and no BP treatment; 12 weeks of home-delivered low-sodium DASH groceries plus dietitian counselling vs stipends for self-directed shopping; adherence by 24-h urine; maintenance assessed at 3 months. Use: the modern real-world delivery test of DASH.
- `Neter2003influence` — MA of 25 RCTs, 4,874 participants. Net −5.1 kg weight loss gave SBP −4.44 (−5.93 to −2.95) and DBP −3.57 (−4.88 to −2.25); per kilogram, −1.05 mmHg SBP (−1.43 to −0.66) and −0.92 DBP. Larger falls when weight loss exceeded 5 kg. Use: the per-kilogram rule of thumb.
- `Roerecke2017effect` — SR/MA, 36 trials, 2,865 participants. No significant BP fall from cutting down in people drinking ≤2 drinks/day; in those drinking ≥6/day who halved intake, SBP −5.50 (−6.70 to −4.30) and DBP −3.97 (−4.70 to −3.25). Threshold effect. Use: alcohol only pays off above a threshold.
- `Blumenthal2010effects` — ENCORE RCT, 144 unmedicated overweight adults with SBP 130–159 / DBP 85–99. Clinic BP −16.1/−9.9 (DASH + weight management), −11.2/−7.5 (DASH alone), −3.4/−3.8 (controls); ambulatory BP followed the same pattern. Use: the ceiling of what a supervised combined programme achieves.
- `Blumenthal2021effects` — TRIUMPH RCT, 140 patients with resistant hypertension (mean 63 years, 59% Black). 4-month supervised diet+exercise: clinic SBP −12.5 (−14.9 to −10.2) vs −7.1 (−10.4 to −3.7) with a single education session; 24-h ambulatory SBP −7.0 vs −0.3. Use: lifestyle still works in the hardest cases, on top of drugs.
- `Group2003effects` — PREMIER RCT, 810 adults with above-optimal BP through stage 1 hypertension (120–159/80–95), unmedicated. Net systolic reduction after subtracting the advice-only group: −3.7 mmHg (established) and −4.3 mmHg (established + DASH) at 6 months. Use: the real-world discount versus feeding/supervised trials.
- `Group2010long` — Look AHEAD, 5,145 overweight/obese adults with type 2 diabetes, 4 years. Intensive lifestyle vs education: weight −6.15% vs −0.88%, SBP −5.33 vs −2.97 mmHg, DBP −2.92 vs −2.48 mmHg averaged over 4 years. Use: what survives at four years is smaller than year one.
- `Tucker2017self` — IPD MA, 25 trials, 7,138–8,292 randomised participants. Self-monitoring cut clinic SBP −3.2 mmHg (−4.9 to −1.6) at 12 months; no effect alone (−1.0, −3.3 to 1.2) but −6.1 mmHg (−9.0 to −3.2) with intensive support; most effective with higher baseline SBP up to 170 mmHg. Use: measuring only helps when attached to support.
- `Neal2021effect` — SSaSS, cluster-randomised, 20,995 people in 600 Chinese villages with stroke history or age ≥60 plus high BP; 75% NaCl / 25% KCl substitute vs regular salt, mean 4.74 years. Stroke rate ratio 0.86 (0.77–0.96), major cardiovascular events 0.87 (0.80–0.94), death 0.88 (0.82–0.95); hyperkalaemia serious adverse events not increased (RR 1.04, 0.80–1.37). Use: the one lifestyle lever with hard outcome evidence.
- `Greenwood2024long` — SR/MA of 16 RCTs (8 with outcomes) of salt substitution ≥6 months; mostly China/Taiwan, older or higher-risk populations. Salt substitute may reduce all-cause mortality (6 RCTs, 27,710 participants). Use: the generalisability caveat on salt substitutes.
