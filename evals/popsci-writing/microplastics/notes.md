# Notes - Are microplastics actually harming our health?

## Review angles retained from the original example

- Direct human evidence: where plastic has been reported in living people and what health links have been observed.
- Experimental plausibility: what cell and animal studies show can happen, without treating laboratory doses as ordinary human exposure.
- Strongest human warning signals: cardiovascular outcomes and gut-disease correlations, with the direction of causation left open.
- Measurement limits and contrary evidence: contamination control, size thresholds, incompatible reporting, and confounding.
- Evidence boundary: whether everyday exposure has been shown to cause illness in otherwise comparable people.

## Close-read papers

- `Tran2026health` - Design/sample: systematic review of 25 studies in living humans, including 14 cross-sectional, nine case-control, one prospective cohort, and one quasi-experimental study. Result: micro- and nanoplastics were reported across cardiovascular, reproductive, gastrointestinal, respiratory, and ocular samples, while associations included inflammation and impaired function. Limitation: most studies were small, methods varied, and confounding plus exposure-measurement bias blocked causal conclusions. Synthesis use: supports the conclusion that plastic is reported inside people while marking the missing causal step.

- `Chartres2024effects` - Design/sample: rapid systematic review including three human observational studies and 28 animal studies across digestive, reproductive, and respiratory outcomes. Result: the authors judged several harms "suspected," with the most complete bodies of evidence supporting reproductive and digestive concerns. Limitation: only three included studies involved humans, so most of the judgment rested on animal evidence and did not establish ordinary human dose-response. Synthesis use: calibrates laboratory and animal harm as a warning signal rather than proof of human illness.

- `Wang2021kidney` - Design/sample: laboratory study of human kidney cells plus male mice, with five mice per group treated orally for four or eight weeks. Result: exposed cells showed stress and inflammatory changes, while treated mice accumulated particles in kidney cells and developed tubular injury and urinary protein leakage. Limitation: the work tested one kind and size of manufactured polystyrene particle in cells and mice, so it cannot show that mixed everyday human exposure causes the same damage. Synthesis use: supplies the concrete experimental mechanism behind biological plausibility.
