## Does creatine help with depression?

**TL;DR** — Maybe a little, and only as an add-on to real treatment like antidepressants or talk therapy. The studies are small, and the true effect might be nothing. Creatine is safe for most people, but it is not a depression treatment on its own.

### The studies say it might help — but only a very little

- Scientists collected 11 studies where about 1,100 people got either creatine or a dummy pill. The creatine group's mood improved a bit more — roughly 2 points on a mood questionnaire, which is less than most people can actually feel [Eckert et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114525105588).
- The same scientists warn that the studies disagree with each other a lot, and that the positive ones look too good to be true. The real effect could be zero [Eckert et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114525105588).
- If you count only people with a diagnosed mental illness, there are just 5 careful studies with 238 people in total. That is very few [Jeryous Fares et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/07067437251408171).

### Creatine looks like a helper, not a rescue

- The two studies with good news gave creatine to people who were starting a treatment that already works. Women starting an antidepressant got better faster with creatine than with the dummy pill [Lyoo et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12010009). People starting talk therapy did clearly better with creatine too [Sherpa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2024.10.004).
- When creatine was given to people whose antidepressant had already failed, it did nothing [Nemets & Levine 2013](https://doi.org/10.1097/yic.0b013e32835ff20f), [Kondo et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00726-016-2194-3).
- The biggest positive study had only 100 people, and its own authors call it an early test, not proof [Sherpa et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroneuro.2024.10.004).

### Creatine does reach the brain — that part is real

- Creatine helps cells store quick energy. Brain scans show that swallowing creatine really does raise the brain's energy stores [Kondo et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00726-016-2194-3), and the brains of people with depression handle this energy a little differently [Harper et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/npp.2016.180).
- In rats, creatine helped the females but made the males worse. That might be why the human studies mostly show benefits in women [Allen et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/npp.2009.160).
- Big surveys find that people who eat more creatine (it is in meat and fish) get depression less often [Bakian et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-020-0741-x), [Ostojic et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/1028415x.2025.2558118). But a survey cannot prove cause and effect — being depressed also changes how much you eat.

### Safe for most people, with one real warning

- Across hundreds of studies, people taking creatine had no more side effects than people taking a dummy pill [Kreider et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2025.2488937). One blood test for the kidneys goes up a little, but the kidneys themselves keep working normally [Tsiaras et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.jrn.2026.04.010).
- The warning: in people with [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder) — an illness where mood swings between very low and dangerously high — a few people flipped into the too-high state while taking creatine [Roitman et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1399-5618.2007.00532.x), [Toniolo et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00702-017-1817-5).

### What we still don't know

- Nobody has ever tested creatine on its own against a dummy pill for depression, and the studies so far are too small to settle anything. Bigger and longer studies, testing men and women separately, are what's missing [Jeryous Fares et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/07067437251408171), [Eckert et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114525105588).
