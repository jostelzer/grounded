EVIDENCE-BACKED STORY
- Sunlight arrives as many visible colors together.
- Clear water droplets, each much larger than a visible-light wave, redirect the colors with similar overall strength.
- Inside a crowd of droplets, light changes direction many times and escapes in many directions as a mixed white glow.
- In a thick cloud, less light reaches the bottom, so the base can look grey even though the droplets remain clear.

DIRECTLY OBSERVED
- Warm-cloud droplets measured in situ have a typical radius near 11 micrometres, many times larger than visible-light wavelengths.
- Cloud optical thickness changes strongly with liquid-water amount and droplet size.
- Cloud brightness and inferred optical thickness vary with three-dimensional shape, sun angle, and viewing angle.

INFERRED, MODEL-SUPPORTED, MIXED, OR UNCERTAIN
- The drawn light paths are a teaching schematic, not tracks of measured individual photons; keep them thin, simple, and clearly representative.
- Do not imply that every color follows an identical route or that water droplets absorb most visible light.

TOPIC-SPECIFIC LAYOUT
- Use one continuous left-to-right scientific mechanism on a white 3:2 landscape canvas, with three lower-case labeled regions rather than boxed cards.
- At left, show three thin parallel red, green, and blue rays grouped as incoming sunlight; they enter a large central cross-section of a pale cloud.
- Build the cloud from many small transparent, outlined spherical water droplets. Show several thin light paths changing direction between droplets without clutter.
- At right and upper edges, show neutral pale rays leaving in several directions as the white glow. At the bottom, use a restrained light-grey shaded region and fewer rays to show that less light arrives there.
- Place every label in quiet white space with a short thin leader. Keep the reading order obvious at phone width.
