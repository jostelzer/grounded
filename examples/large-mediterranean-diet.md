## What does the Mediterranean diet actually do for health — and how strong is the evidence?

**TL;DR** — The benefits are real but mostly modest, and they are firmest for the heart: randomised trials show roughly 20–30% fewer major cardiovascular events, and large cohorts consistently link higher adherence to about 10–25% lower mortality. Diabetes prevention is probable; effects on depression, liver fat, weight, and some cancers are promising but rest on small or observational studies; for cognition the cohorts say yes while the biggest trial said no. The evidence base has known soft spots — most of it is observational, effects shrink outside Mediterranean countries, and the flagship trial had to be retracted and republished — but the corrected result survived.

### "Mediterranean diet" is a scored pattern, not one menu

- Research definitions converge on: abundant vegetables, fruit, legumes, and unrefined cereals; olive oil as the main fat (a high ratio of [monounsaturated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monounsaturated_fat) to saturated fat); regular fish; little red or processed meat; moderate dairy; moderate wine with meals [Bach et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1079/phn2005936), [Menotti & Puddu 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcdd12040141).
- Adherence is measured with a priori scores — the original 0–8/0–9 Trichopoulou scale and its descendants, plus the 14-item Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener (MEDAS) used in trials [Trichopoulou et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.311.7018.1457), [Bach et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1079/phn2005936).
- Dozens of score variants exist, with different components and cut-offs; this heterogeneity complicates every [meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis) in the field [Bach et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1079/phn2005936), [Dinu et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejcn.2017.58).

### The claim began as ecology, not trials: the Seven Countries Study

- In the [Seven Countries Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Countries_Study) (15 cohorts, 11,579 men, 15-year mortality), coronary death rates were lowest in cohorts where olive oil was the main fat, and the monounsaturated:saturated fat ratio tracked all-cause and coronary mortality across cohorts — an ecological association, with no causal claim made [Keys et al. 1986](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a114480).
- [Ancel Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancel_Keys)' books and these cohort contrasts defined the diet and set the research agenda for the next half-century [Menotti & Puddu 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcdd12040141), [Blackburn 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwx071).
- Later re-analyses of the study's food data produced adherence scores whose ecological correlation with 50-year coronary mortality across cohorts reached −0.91 — still ecology, but remarkably consistent ecology [Menotti & Puddu 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcdd12040141).

### Randomised trials show fewer cardiovascular events — in three different settings

- Primary prevention: in PREDIMED (7,447 high-risk adults, 4.8 years), major cardiovascular events occurred in 3.8% (olive-oil arm) and 3.4% (nut arm) vs 4.4% (low-fat advice); [hazard ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_ratio) 0.69 (95% [CI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval) 0.53–0.91) and 0.72 (0.54–0.95) [Estruch et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa1800389).
- Secondary prevention: the Lyon Diet Heart Study (605 post-infarction patients, 46 months) reported cardiac death plus nonfatal infarction in 14 vs 44 patients, adjusted risk ratios 0.28–0.53 — strikingly large, never replicated at that magnitude [de Lorgeril et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.cir.99.6.779).
- Secondary prevention again: CORDIOPREV (1,002 coronary patients, 7 years) found 87 vs 111 major events on Mediterranean vs low-fat diet (28.1 vs 37.7 per 1,000 person-years; adjusted hazard ratios 0.72–0.75), with the benefit evident in men and not demonstrated in the 175 women [Delgado-Lista et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(22)00122-2).
- Pooling [RCTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trial): composite cardiovascular events fell (RR 0.63, 95% CI 0.53–0.75) but all-cause mortality did not (RR 1.00, 0.86–1.15) [Liyanage et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0159252).

### PREDIMED was retracted and republished — and the corrected estimate barely moved

- A statistical screen of baseline data in 5,087 trials flagged improbable distributions, prompting scrutiny of PREDIMED's randomisation [Carlisle 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/anae.13938).
- The investigators then found protocol deviations — enrolment of household members without randomisation and misapplied randomisation at two of 11 sites — withdrew the 2013 report, and republished in 2018 with analyses not assuming full randomisation [Estruch et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa1800389).
- Omitting the 1,588 participants with known or suspected departures left the effect essentially unchanged (hazard ratios 0.69 and 0.72) [Estruch et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa1800389).
- Two durable caveats survive the correction: the composite result was driven mainly by stroke (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.45–0.80; about 24 down to 14 strokes per 1,000), and the trial showed no effect on total mortality (HR 1.0, 0.81–1.24), for which it was not powered [Rees et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd009825.pub3), [Guasch-Ferré et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.numecd.2017.05.004).

### Independent syntheses back the cardiovascular benefit at moderate certainty — Cochrane stays cooler

- A [network meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_meta-analysis) of 40 trials (35,548 patients) rated Mediterranean programmes superior to minimal intervention with moderate certainty ([GRADE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRADE_approach)): all-cause mortality OR 0.72 (95% CI 0.56–0.92), cardiovascular mortality 0.55 (0.39–0.78), stroke 0.65 (0.46–0.93), nonfatal infarction 0.48 (0.36–0.65) — about 17 fewer deaths per 1,000 over 5 years at intermediate risk [Karam et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2022-072003).
- The same analysis found no convincing advantage over low-fat programmes for mortality or infarction [Karam et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2022-072003).
- An [umbrella review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_review) of RCT meta-analyses put cardiovascular risk ratios at 0.55–0.64 [Chareonrungrueangchai et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12041088).
- The Cochrane review (30 RCTs, 12,461 participants) still concludes "some uncertainty": risk-factor effects are small — systolic blood pressure −3.0 mmHg (95% CI −3.5 to −2.5), total cholesterol −0.16 mmol/L (−0.32 to 0.00) — and evidence quality low to moderate throughout [Rees et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd009825.pub3).

### Large cohorts agree on lower mortality, with a dose–response — but the effect is smaller than early studies suggested

- The founding cohort: in 22,043 Greeks, each 2-point adherence increment predicted 25% lower total mortality (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.64–0.87) [Trichopoulou et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa025039); across 74,607 elderly Europeans the same increment predicted 8% (3–12%) [Trichopoulou et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38415.644155.8f).
- Meta-analyses of cohorts converge near HR 0.90 per 2 points (0.89–0.91; 29 studies, 1.68 million people) and 0.79 (0.77–0.81) for highest-vs-lowest adherence [Sofi et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.a1344), [Soltani et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/advances/nmz041), [Eleftheriou et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0007114518002593); the newest synthesis (54 cohorts, 1.83 million) grades the evidence moderate at RR 0.96 (0.95–0.97) per single point [Nucci et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nut.2026.113189).
- US cohorts agree: top-quintile Alternate Mediterranean scores predicted mortality HR 0.82 (0.79–0.84) over up to 36 years in the [Nurses' Health Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurses%27_Health_Study) and Health Professionals cohorts, and CVD HR 0.83 (0.79–0.86) [Shan et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.6117), [Shan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2020.2176); in 25,315 US women followed up to 25 years, HR 0.77 (0.70–0.84) [Ahmad et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.14322).
- It also holds in older adults (all-cause mortality RR 0.77, 0.70–0.83) and in people who already have diabetes (2-point HR 0.63; pooled RR 0.93, 0.90–0.97) [Furbatto et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu16223947), [Bonaccio et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487315569409), [Muscogiuri et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nut.2025.112975).
- Component analyses attribute most of the association to moderate alcohol (~24%), low meat (~17%), and high vegetable intake (~16%) — not to any single "superfood" [Trichopoulou et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b2337).

### Diabetes is where trial and cohort evidence line up best

- In PREDIMED, the pooled Mediterranean arms cut incident diabetes (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.54–0.92), and yearly-measured adherence showed a gradient: HR 0.80 (0.70–0.92) per 2 points, 0.46 (0.25–0.83) for highest vs lowest [Bloomfield et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.7326/m16-0361), [Martínez-González et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-023-01994-2).
- Adding energy restriction and exercise to the diet helps more: PREDIMED-Plus (4,746 adults, 6 years) cut diabetes incidence a further 31% (95% CI 18–41%) vs unrestricted Mediterranean diet — absolute risk 9.5% vs 12.0% [Ruiz-Canela et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7326/annals-25-00388).
- In established diabetes, meta-analyses of RCTs show better glycaemic control ([HbA1c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycated_hemoglobin) −0.30 to −0.47%) and [BMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_mass_index) −0.83 (95% CI −1.40 to −0.26); long-term trials also show a 49% higher probability of remission from the [metabolic syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_syndrome) [Esposito et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008222), [Wu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17243908).
- An objective check: a blood biomarker score of Mediterranean eating (carotenoids + fatty acids, validated in a feeding trial) predicted 29% lower diabetes incidence per SD (HR 0.71, 0.65–0.77) in 22,202 [EPIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Prospective_Investigation_into_Cancer_and_Nutrition)-InterAct participants — larger than self-report suggests, arguing the cohort signal is not just reporting bias [Sobiecki et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1004221).
- A Mediterranean lifestyle score also predicted 30% lower diabetes incidence in the non-Mediterranean [UK Biobank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Biobank) (top-quartile HR 0.70, 0.62–0.79) [Maroto-Rodriguez et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-023-01999-x).

### Risk factors and weight improve consistently — but modestly, and olive-oil-supplement trials disappoint

- Meta-analyses of trials and cohorts show small improvements across metabolic syndrome components: waist −0.42 cm, triglycerides −6.1 mg/dl, systolic BP −2.4 mmHg, glucose −3.9 mg/dl; pooled metabolic syndrome risk RR ~0.81 [Kastorini et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2010.09.073), [Godos et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1080/09637486.2016.1221900), [Papadaki et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12113342).
- For weight, the Mediterranean diet beat low fat (−4.4 vs −2.9 kg at 2 years) and matched low carbohydrate (−4.7 kg) in the DIRECT trial [Shai et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa0708681); a network meta-analysis found it the only named diet whose cardiovascular risk-factor benefits persisted at 12 months [Ge et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.m696).
- Body composition: energy-restricted Mediterranean diet plus activity reduced total and visceral fat and attenuated lean-mass loss over 3 years [Konieczna et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.37994); a [polyphenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphenol)-enriched "green" version more than doubled visceral-fat loss (−14.1% vs −6.0%) [Zelicha et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-022-02525-8).
- The contrary case: a 2024 meta-analysis of 18 trials of Mediterranean diet enriched with olive oil found no consistent benefit on any cardiometabolic marker except triglycerides (−2.4 mg/dl, 95% CI −4.5 to −0.3), and a GRADE-based review in overweight adults rated most risk-factor evidence very low quality [Keshani et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-024-03478-9), [Hernandez et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/27697061.2024.2440051).

### Cancer: broad but shallow observational protection; trial evidence is a single fragile signal

- Cohort meta-analyses associate highest adherence with lower cancer mortality (RR 0.86–0.87), colorectal (0.82–0.83), and more weakly breast cancer (0.94, 95% CI 0.90–0.97) [Morze et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-020-02346-6), [Schwingshackl et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9101063).
- An umbrella review rates the evidence robust only for overall cancer incidence and mortality; for most site-specific cancers it is suggestive or weak, and null for several sites [Dinu et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejcn.2017.58), [Morze et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-020-02346-6).
- The only randomised signal: PREDIMED's olive-oil arm had fewer invasive breast cancers (HR 0.32, 95% CI 0.13–0.79) — a secondary outcome with just 35 cases, needing confirmation [Toledo et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.4838).
- Olive oil itself, at +25 g/day, is associated with lower CVD, diabetes, and mortality but **not** cancer (RR 0.94, 0.86–1.03) across 1.29 million participants [Martínez-González et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2022.10.001).

### Cognition is the sharpest trial–cohort disagreement in the field

- Cohorts and their meta-analyses are positive: cognitive impairment HR 0.82 (0.75–0.89), dementia 0.89 (0.83–0.95), Alzheimer's 0.70 (0.60–0.82) [Fekete et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-024-01488-3), [Fu et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2022.946361); in 60,298 UK Biobank participants, top-tertile adherence predicted 23% lower dementia risk (HR 0.77, 0.65–0.91) independent of polygenic risk [Shannon et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-023-02772-3); the related MIND diet shows pooled dementia HR 0.83 (0.76–0.90) [Chen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2023.0800).
- Small trial evidence agreed: PREDIMED's Navarra sub-study found better [MMSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini%E2%80%93Mental_State_Examination) scores after 6.5 years (+0.62, 95% CI 0.18–1.05, vs control), and pooled RCTs show episodic-memory [SMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardized_mean_difference) 0.20 (0.09–0.30) [Martínez-Lapiscina et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2012-304792), [Fu et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2022.946361).
- Then the largest dedicated trial came back null: the 3-year MIND trial (604 older adults, both arms calorie-restricted) found a global-cognition difference of 0.035 SD (95% CI −0.022 to 0.092, p=0.23) and no MRI differences — both groups improved and both lost ~5 kg [Barnes et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2302368).
- The likeliest reconciliations: an active, weight-losing control arm, a well-nourished trial population, and residual confounding in the cohorts; the trial cannot rule out effects of longer duration or higher-risk populations, but it caps expectations for 3-year effects in healthy volunteers [Barnes et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2302368), [Maki et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3945/an.113.004929).

### Depression: three small trials with large effects, and a meta-analysis that urges caution

- Cohorts associate Mediterranean adherence with 33% lower incident depression (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.55–0.82) [Lassale et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-018-0237-8).
- Pooled across 5 RCTs (1,507 participants): depressive symptoms fell by SMD −0.53 (95% CI −0.90 to −0.16) — but [heterogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Study_heterogeneity) is high (I² = 87%), the [prediction interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prediction_interval) spans zero (−1.86 to 0.81), and certainty is rated low [Bizzozero-Peroni et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuad176).

- All three trials are small, unblinded to participants, and use comparators (befriending, social groups) that may underperform genuine usual care — reasons the pooled certainty stays low despite consistent direction [Bizzozero-Peroni et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuad176).

### Liver, frailty, and joints: promising, unevenly supported

- Fatty liver: meta-analysis of 6 small RCTs shows reduced steatosis (fatty-liver index SMD −1.06, 95% CI −1.95 to −0.17) and insulin resistance [Kawaguchi et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0041-1723751); a crossover trial confirmed steatosis reduction without weight loss [Ryan et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2013.02.012); the 18-month DIRECT-PLUS trial nearly doubled intrahepatic-fat loss with a green-Mediterranean variant (−38.9% vs −12.2% proportional loss vs guidelines) [Yaskolka Meir et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2020-323106) — but one 12-week RCT found no advantage over low fat, and reviews still put calorie restriction, not diet composition, at the centre of [NAFLD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-alcoholic_fatty_liver_disease) care [George et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/liv.15264), [Haigh et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2022.06.037).
- Frailty: higher adherence predicts roughly half the incident [frailty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frailty_syndrome) risk in Spanish cohorts (ORs 0.48 and 0.38 for top adherence) and lower frailty plus 8-year mortality in US NHANES [León-Muñoz et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2014.06.013), [Maroto-Rodriguez et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glab292), [Jayanama et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-021-01918-5).
- Rheumatoid arthritis: systematic review finds improvement in some disease-activity measures — smaller and less consistent than for omega-3 supplementation [Philippou et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuaa033).

### The mechanisms are measurable, not just plausible

- Endothelium: in CORDIOPREV, one year of Mediterranean diet produced higher [flow-mediated dilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow-mediated_dilation) than low fat (3.83%, 95% CI 2.91–4.23, vs 1.16%, 0.80–1.98) [Yubero-Serrano et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003282), and by 5–7 years carotid [intima–media thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intima%E2%80%93media_thickness) regressed (−0.027 mm) while the low-fat arm's did not [Jimenez-Torres et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1161/strokeaha.120.033214).
- Inflammation: top-quintile Mediterranean scores predict ~24% lower [C-reactive protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-reactive_protein) [Fung et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn.82.1.163); in the Women's Health Study, inflammation and small-molecule metabolites were the largest single mediators of the mortality association [Ahmad et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.14322).
- [Gut microbiota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_microbiota): one-year interventions in five countries shifted taxa toward short-chain-fatty-acid producers, tracking lower CRP/IL-17 and frailty markers [Ghosh et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2019-319654); 8-week and 1-year trials confirm microbiome and bile-acid shifts with improved insulin sensitivity [Meslier et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2019-320438), [Muralidharan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqab150), [Rinott et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-022-01015-z).
- Aging biology: the polyphenol-rich green-Mediterranean variant attenuated [epigenetic-clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetic_clock) aging and age-related brain atrophy in participants over 50 [Yaskolka Meir et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-023-03067-3), [Kaplan et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqac001).

### Why the headline numbers may still flatter the diet

- Nearly all mortality evidence is observational — a Dietary Guidelines systematic review found 152 [cohort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohort_study)-type studies and one RCT — and adherent eaters exercise more, smoke less, and are richer; adjustment cannot remove all of this [English et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.22277), [Maki et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3945/an.113.004929), [Ioannidis 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.11025).
- Effects shrink with distance from the Mediterranean: pooled mortality HR 0.82 in Mediterranean countries vs 0.92 elsewhere per 2 points [Soltani et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/advances/nmz041).
- Self-reported [food-frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_frequency_questionnaire) measurement is noisy, and dozens of non-interchangeable adherence scores are in use [Bach et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1079/phn2005936), [Ioannidis 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.11025).
- Trial effects depend on intensive support (free foods, dietitian counselling, quarterly sessions), so real-world adoption will likely deliver less [Rees et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd009825.pub3), [Karam et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2022-072003); some trials are part-funded by olive-oil sector foundations [Delgado-Lista et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(22)00122-2).
- The umbrella-review verdict is a fair summary: robust evidence for mortality, cardiovascular disease, overall cancer incidence, neurodegenerative disease, and diabetes; suggestive or weak for most everything else [Dinu et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/ejcn.2017.58).

### What would settle the open questions

- Hard-endpoint results from PREDIMED-Plus (6,874 participants, 8-year cardiovascular follow-up of energy-restricted Mediterranean diet plus exercise) will test whether adding weight loss amplifies the PREDIMED effect [Konieczna et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.37994), [Ruiz-Canela et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.7326/annals-25-00388).
- Objective adherence biomarkers (metabolomic and carotenoid/fatty-acid scores) can cut the reporting bias that plagues cohort estimates [Sobiecki et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1004221), [Martínez-González et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-023-01994-2).
- Polyphenol-dense variants (green-Mediterranean) are testing whether the pattern can be improved rather than merely exported [Yaskolka Meir et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1136/gutjnl-2020-323106), [Zelicha et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-022-02525-8).
- Cognition and depression need trials that are longer, larger, and run in higher-risk or non-Mediterranean populations, with credible control arms — the MIND trial shows how a strong control can erase an expected effect [Barnes et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2302368), [Bizzozero-Peroni et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuad176), [Wu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17243908).
