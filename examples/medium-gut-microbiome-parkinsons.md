## Does the gut microbiome contribute causally to Parkinson’s disease, and is it clinically actionable?

**TL;DR** — Parkinson’s disease is associated with a recurring but small and highly context-dependent stool-microbe pattern. Animal experiments support several causal routes, but human evidence does not yet show that microbiome changes initiate or accelerate Parkinson’s disease; probiotics can improve constipation, whereas effects of stool transfer on motor disease are inconsistent.

### Broad stool signatures recur, but no disease-specific fingerprint does

- Reanalysis of ten [gut-microbiome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_microbiota) [16S ribosomal RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16S_ribosomal_RNA) datasets repeatedly found more *Akkermansia*, *Lactobacillus* and *Bifidobacterium* and fewer Lachnospiraceae/*Faecalibacterium*, yet between-study origin dominated community structure and the Parkinson’s effect was small; a 1,703-person meta-analysis likewise found study and geography explained the largest variation. [Romano et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41531-021-00156-z), [Toh et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2021.11.017), [Nishiwaki et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.28119), [Bai et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2024.112444)
- [Metagenomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metagenomics) results are not a universal taxonomic signature: one four-cohort analysis retained only *Intestinimonas butyriciproducens* consistently, although functional gene patterns recurred; a separate uniform US cohort found widespread differences but could not test portability. [Boktor et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.29300), [Wallen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34667-x)
- [Alpha diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_diversity) was null across seven datasets (631 Parkinson’s, 436 controls). Diagnostic models averaged **71.9%** within-study [receiver-operating-characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receiver_operating_characteristic) area under the curve (AUC), but only **61%** across studies; multi-study training improved leave-one-study-out AUC to 68%, still inadequate for screening. [Plassais et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcab113), [Romano et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56829-3)

| Study | Design and sample | Main result | What limits inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scheperjans 2015 | 16S case-control, n=144 | Prevotellaceae 77.6% lower; classifier sensitivity 66.7%, specificity 90.3% [Scheperjans et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.26069) | Single population; constipation entered classifier |
| Wallen 2020 | Two internally replicated cohorts, n=840 | Fifteen genera replicated in three correlated clusters [Wallen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41531-020-0112-6) | Cross-sectional; medication/consequence unresolved |
| Toh 2022 | Ten-cohort raw-data meta-analysis, n=1,703 | Marginal disease effect; study and geography dominated [Toh et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2021.11.017) | Heterogeneous collection and populations |
| Boktor 2023 | Two new US cohorts plus Germany/China | One species consistent across all four cohorts; functions more stable [Boktor et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.29300) | n=244 newly sequenced; cross-sectional |
| Romano 2025 | Machine-learning meta-analysis, n=4,489 | External AUC 61%; multi-study AUC 68% [Romano et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56829-3) | No clinical prospective validation |

### Prodromal associations appear before diagnosis, but prospective conversion evidence is missing

- Among 666 at-risk older adults, constipation, inactivity and possible [rapid-eye-movement sleep behavior disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_eye_movement_sleep_behavior_disorder) (RBD) tracked community composition, but olfactory loss, autonomic features, family history and aggregate prodromal probability did not. [Heinzel et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.26128)
- A cross-sectional series (108 controls, 127 RBD relatives, 170 RBD, 36 early Parkinson’s) found Parkinson-like changes in RBD after adjustment, but disease status explained only 3.5% of community variation; a later spouse-controlled cohort reported RBD-dependent microbial dynamics, not incident Parkinson’s. [Huang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38248-4), [Lee et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-025-02095-w)
- Over 12 months, 74 patients retained depletion of several butyrate-producing taxa versus 74 household controls, but microbiome-plus-diet prediction of faster progression rose only from AUC 0.58 to 0.64. This is longitudinal after diagnosis, not proof that dysbiosis precedes conversion. [Lubomski et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2022.875261)

### Constipation, medication, diet, geography and laboratory choices can mimic disease effects

- In 327 participants, Parkinson’s status had an independent association, but region, age, sex, fruit/vegetable intake, catechol-*O*-methyltransferase inhibitors, anticholinergics and possibly levodopa also had distinct signals. A five-country meta-analysis similarly found that the inhibitor strongly increased Lactobacillaceae. [Hill‐Burns et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.26942), [Nishiwaki et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.28119)
- In a 300-person study, disease-associated shifts persisted after adjustment, yet stool consistency and constipation strongly tracked taxa and proteolytic metabolites. Thus transit can be confounder, mediator, or consequence; statistical adjustment cannot determine which. [Cirstea et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.28052)
- Sixteen early studies reported more than 100 differential taxa while varying in transport, extraction, sequencing region, pipeline and covariates. Even an early “L-DOPA-naïve” metagenomic study had only 31 cases and most used other dopaminergic drugs. [Boertien et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3233/jpd-191711), [Bedarf et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-017-0428-y)

### Barrier, inflammation and metabolite findings are plausible but compartment-dependent

- A small untreated study (9 patients, 10 controls) found approximately doubled whole-gut sucralose permeability, but small-intestinal sugar probes and serum endotoxin were null. A 2025 critical review found incompatible assays and concluded that consistent [intestinal permeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestinal_permeability) impairment is not established. [Forsyth et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0028032), [Derkinderen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2025.105796)
- Fecal [short-chain fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-chain_fatty_acid) (SCFAs) were lower in two small case-control studies and related to stool inflammation, sex and taxa; plasma SCFAs were not lower and acetate was higher after adjustment. Stool production, absorption and circulating exposure therefore cannot be treated as one measure. [Unger et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2016.08.019), [Aho et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13024-021-00427-6), [Shin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.28016)
- Multi-omics studies associate Parkinson’s with altered fecal SCFAs, sulfur metabolism, secondary bile acids and circulating microbial metabolites, but medication, constipation and disease severity remain alternative causes. These data nominate pathways; they do not show that a metabolite drives neurodegeneration. [Tan et al. 2021a](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.25982), [Hertel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2019.10.035), [Li et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo11010029), [Chen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3233/jpd-223179)

### Gut-to-brain [α-synuclein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-synuclein) propagation is experimentally strong in mice and indirect in humans

- In α-synuclein-overexpressing mice, microbes were required for motor deficits and neuroinflammation; antibiotics improved pathology and Parkinson’s-donor microbiota worsened it. Antibiotic depletion also protected a toxin-based mouse model. [Sampson et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.018), [Pu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.18632/aging.102221)
- Injecting pathological α-synuclein into mouse gut produced staged vagal spread, neurodegeneration and symptoms prevented by truncal vagotomy; engineered intestinal epithelial expression likewise transferred seeding activity to vagus and hindbrain. Both are powerful model tests, not spontaneous human disease. [Kim et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.05.035), [Chandra et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.172192)
- Human evidence is compatible with more than one route: phosphorylated α-synuclein appeared in colonic nerves in 4/17 people with isolated RBD, 1/19 with Parkinson’s and 0/14 controls, while non-specific staining was nearly ubiquitous; imaging supports distinct “body-first” and “brain-first” subtypes. [Sprenger et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1212/wnl.0000000000002126), [Horsager et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awaa238)
- Truncal vagotomy after >20 years was associated with lower Parkinson’s risk ([hazard ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_ratio) 0.53, 95% [CI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval) 0.28–0.99), and large appendectomy cohorts reported modest risk reductions; surgical indication, era and residual confounding prevent causal interpretation. [Svensson et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.24448), [Killinger et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aar5280), [Liu et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2020.00510)

### Human causality tests remain weaker than the animal perturbations

- Bidirectional [Mendelian randomization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_randomization) reported 12 suggestive taxon associations; genetically predicted *Bifidobacterium* had an [odds ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odds_ratio) of 0.77 (95% CI 0.60–0.99), while Parkinson’s liability also predicted microbial differences. Taxon-level instruments were weak and results conflict with some observational SCFA patterns. [Jiang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.15848)
- Animal antibiotics, microbe-free conditions and donor transfers show that microbiota can modify engineered phenotypes, but they neither identify a necessary human organism nor establish that antibiotics would prevent Parkinson’s. [Sampson et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.018), [Pu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.18632/aging.102221)

### Microbiome-targeted treatment helps constipation; motor outcomes are inconsistent

| Intervention | Design | Motor result | Gastrointestinal/safety result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Donor [fecal microbiota transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecal_microbiota_transplant) (FMT) | Double-blind phase 2, n=46 | Off-medication motor change −5.8 vs −2.7 at 12 months; p=0.0235 [Bruggeman et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102563) | Transit favored FMT; most secondary outcomes null |
| Single-dose FMT | Four-centre RCT, n=48 | Difference 0.97 points (95% CI −5.10 to 7.03) at 6 months [Scheperjans et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2024.2305) | Gastrointestinal adverse events 53% vs 7% |
| Oral FMT | Randomized trial, n=56 | Total score group×time −6.56 (95% CI −12.98 to −0.13) at 12 weeks [Cheng et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2023.2284247) | GI scores improved; baseline imbalance/unmasked investigators |
| Single-dose FMT | Double-blind RCT, n=59 | Difference 1.50 (95% CI −4.28 to 7.28) at 12 months [Figura et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78153) | Some time-specific nonmotor changes |
| Repeated donor FMT | Double-blind phase 2, n=72 drug-naïve | Motor change −3.8 vs +0.1 at 35 weeks [Zhang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-026-02604-9) | Constipation −6.5 vs −0.7; mild self-limited events |

- Unretracted probiotic/prebiotic trials consistently improved bowel frequency over 4–8 weeks: between-group gains were 1.1 complete movements/week (n=120) and 1.3 spontaneous movements/week (n=72); another trial shortened transit by 36 hours but changed neither motor nor nonmotor scores. [Barichella et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1212/wnl.0000000000003127), [Tan et al. 2021b](https://doi.org/10.1212/wnl.0000000000010998), [Ibrahim et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0244680)
- Diet evidence is preliminary: an uncontrolled five-week Mediterranean-diet pilot in eight patients improved constipation and changed *Roseburia*, without changing permeability. [Rusch et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.794640)

### Clinical use should remain symptom-directed while causality and reproducibility are tested

- No stool profile is validated for diagnosis, prognosis or treatment selection, and neither probiotics nor FMT has established disease-modifying efficacy. Probiotics are supported for constipation; FMT remains experimental because motor effects, donor response, dosing and adverse events vary. [Romano et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56829-3), [Tan et al. 2021b](https://doi.org/10.1212/wnl.0000000000010998), [Scheperjans et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2024.2305), [Figura et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78153)
- Decisive studies need incident prodromal cohorts, repeated transit and diet measurements, absolute microbial loads, medication-naïve sampling, harmonized multicountry metagenomics/metabolomics, and preregistered trials with independent replication and motor progression—not just stool composition—as the primary endpoint. [Boertien et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3233/jpd-191711), [Derkinderen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2025.105796), [Huang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38248-4)
