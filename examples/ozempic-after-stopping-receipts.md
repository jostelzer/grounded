## Outcome certainty (separate from source support)

- Semaglutide exposure fades gradually because the molecule has a long half-life that supports weekly dosing.: moderate — Drug exposure declines gradually; clinical pharmacokinetic studies support long elimination but do not measure post-cessation symptoms.
- While semaglutide is present, randomized human studies consistently find lower appetite or food intake.: moderate — Placebo-controlled human experiments consistently support on-treatment appetite suppression; post-cessation hunger is inferred.
- Delayed gastric emptying was not detected after 20 weeks of semaglutide in two randomized studies.: moderate — Two studies found no late treatment delay with an indirect test; this cannot exclude early or individual gastric effects.
- Continued semaglutide produces large average weight loss over 68 to 104 weeks in obesity trials.: high — Large randomized trials consistently show sustained average loss during treatment.
- In randomized withdrawal, switching from semaglutide to placebo produced weight regain while continued treatment produced further loss.: high — Randomized withdrawal directly identifies the continuation-versus-switch contrast among dose-tolerant participants.
- One year after semaglutide withdrawal in STEP 1, participants had regained about two-thirds of their prior weight loss but remained below baseline on average.: moderate — The direct extension measures one-year regain but selected follow-up and withdrawal of lifestyle support limit attribution.
- Across randomized GLP-1-receptor-agonist studies, weight regain after cessation is consistent but highly heterogeneous.: moderate — Regain direction is consistent; pooled magnitudes differ too much for a universal estimate.
- The newest synthesis estimates faster early regain after newer incretins, but its longer-term trajectory is a projection.: moderate — Observed early regain is supported; longer curves are extrapolations beyond observed modern-drug follow-up.
- Weight regain after semaglutide withdrawal is accompanied by deterioration in several cardiometabolic markers.: moderate — Several measured markers deteriorate after withdrawal; secondary analyses are not independent studies.
- After GLP-1-receptor-agonist cessation, pooled HbA1c rose more in type 2 diabetes than in obesity cohorts.: moderate — Pooled HbA1c rises after cessation but magnitude varies substantially across settings.
- Continuous semaglutide reduced major cardiovascular events in high-risk adults without diabetes, but withdrawal trials have not measured whether that event benefit persists after stopping.: moderate — SELECT supports benefit under randomized assignment; evidence for persistence after cessation is insufficient.
- In routine US care, discontinuation within one year is common and restarting after discontinuation is also frequent.: moderate — Large care cohorts document stopping and restarting, but recording definitions and populations differ.
- Cost or insurance problems are the most frequently documented reason for stopping semaglutide or tirzepatide in one clinical-practice sample.: low — Chart review identifies common recorded reasons in one selected health system.
- In routine care, longer medication coverage is associated with greater one-year weight reduction.: low — Coverage is associated with weight outcomes but confounding prevents full causal attribution.
- Exercise begun during liraglutide treatment improved weight and fat maintenance one year after both interventions ended.: moderate — Randomized initial allocation supports a maintenance benefit, with important drug and attrition boundaries.
- A small polycystic-ovary-syndrome cohort continuing metformin regained about one-third of its semaglutide-induced loss over two years.: very_low — Uncontrolled PCOS cohort describes a selected trajectory and cannot identify a metformin effect.
- A selected type 2 diabetes cohort maintained weight after GLP-1 deprescribing while continuing carbohydrate-restricted telemedicine care.: low — Matched program cohort suggests maintenance is possible but selection and co-interventions prevent causal attribution.
- Evidence for a reliable lifestyle protocol that prevents post-semaglutide regain remains incomplete.: low — Promising maintenance clues have not established a portable semaglutide cessation protocol.
- Existing studies cannot predict an individual person's amount or timing of weight regain after stopping semaglutide.: low — Documented heterogeneity prevents treating group averages as personal forecasts.
- Ozempic semaglutide identity: high — The formulation and historical indication are directly recorded.
