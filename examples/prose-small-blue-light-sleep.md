## Does blue light before bed ruin sleep?

**Abstract** — Evening light rich in short wavelengths can suppress the hormonal signal of biological night and delay the body clock, but “ruins sleep” is too strong. Controlled experiments show the clearest sleep effects after long, bright, near-bedtime exposure. Shorter or dimmer screen exposures often alter melatonin without changing whole-night sleep, and daytime light history modifies the response. Real-world screen use can still reduce sleep when it continues in bed, although delayed bedtime, engaging content, and interaction cannot be separated from light. Blue-light filters change the optical stimulus and sometimes improve subjective sleep, yet randomized trials have not shown dependable objective benefits in healthy adults. The best-supported conclusion is conditional: reducing bright evening light may help, particularly for sensitive people or those with sleep problems, but blue wavelength alone is neither a universal cause of major sleep loss nor an established stand-alone treatment target.

### Introduction

The popular claim combines three distinct questions: whether short-wavelength light changes circadian biology, whether that change impairs the next sleep episode, and whether filtering the light improves sleep. These outcomes are not interchangeable. A screen can suppress [melatonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melatonin) or shift the internal clock without measurably shortening sleep, while screen use also bundles light with delayed bedtime, content, and interaction. [Cajochen et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00165.2011), [Blume et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsac199)

### Circadian mechanism

The circadian response is dose dependent. Narrow-band 469-nm light produced graded melatonin suppression in eight adults, and a synthesis of 19 laboratory datasets found that illumination weighted for [melanopsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanopsin) sensitivity best predicted circadian responses across spectra, intensities, and durations. [West et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01413.2009), [Brown 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpi.12655) Five hours at a blue-enriched computer display also suppressed melatonin and sleepiness in 13 men, but subsequent sleep was not measured. [Cajochen et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00165.2011)

Melatonin suppression is not a proxy for sleep damage. In a preregistered crossover experiment, one hour of visually matched light at 123 versus 59 melanopic lux suppressed melatonin by about 14%, yet changed neither sleepiness nor any [polysomnography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysomnography) outcome (n=29). [Blume et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsac199) The mechanism is real, but the marker does not establish sleep loss.

### Controlled screen exposure

Controlled experiments show a dose-and-timing pattern rather than universal damage:

| Exposure | Design | Objective sleep result |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum-brightness e-reader vs print, 4 h before bed for 5 evenings | Inpatient crossover, n=12 | Sleep onset was **9.9 min later** and rapid-eye-movement sleep **11.8 min shorter**; total sleep and efficiency were unchanged. [Chang et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418490112) |
| Tablet vs print, 2 h after bright daytime light | Crossover, n=14 | **No difference** in melatonin or recorded sleep. [Rångtell et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2016.06.016) |
| Higher- vs lower-melanopic display light, 3.5 h | Four-intensity crossover, n=72 men | Sleep latency shortened only at the brightest setting; duration and architecture were unchanged. [Schöllhorn et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-04598-4) |
| Smartphone filter on/off vs print, 90 min | Crossover, 33 male adolescents and 35 male young adults | Whole-night sleep was unchanged; adults alone had slightly less early deep sleep without the filter. [Höhn et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcae173) |

The largest effect followed the longest, brightest, near-bedtime schedule, whereas shorter or dimmer exposures were null or limited. [Chang et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1418490112), [Rångtell et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2016.06.016), [Schöllhorn et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-04598-4) Spectrum therefore acts alongside intensity, duration, timing, and prior light. [Rångtell et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2016.06.016), [Schöllhorn et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-04598-4) The smartphone comparison also found no general whole-night benefit from its software filter. [Höhn et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcae173)

### Real-world exposure and individual differences

Naturalistic evidence points to behaviour as another pathway. In 79 youths, screen use during the two hours before bed was not associated with total sleep; every additional 10 minutes of interactive use in bed was associated with 9 minutes less sleep (95% [confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval), 2–16 minutes less). [Brosnan et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2024.2914) The observational design could not separate light from delayed sleep, gaming, or multitasking.

Sensitivity varies too widely for a universal threshold. Among 55 adults, the estimated light level producing half-maximal melatonin suppression ranged from 6 to 350 lux. [Phillips et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1901824116) Home monitoring found 0%–87% suppression under an average home’s light, while a six-condition crossover found moderately repeatable differences in 22 people. [Cain et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-75622-4), [Santhi et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-079x.2011.00970.x)

### Blue-light filters

Trials do not establish blue-blocking glasses as a reliable treatment. A Cochrane review found three positive and three null randomized sleep trials (n=148) and rated certainty very low. [Singh et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.cd013244.pub2) A 2025 [actigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actigraphy) meta-analysis (three crossover trials, n=49) estimated sleep onset 4.86 minutes earlier (95% interval −20.23 to +10.52) and total sleep 8.75 minutes longer (−35.31 to +52.82), with no improvement in sleep efficiency or wake after sleep onset. [Luna-Rangel et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1699303)

In 20 healthy adults, glasses improved selected self-reports but no objective outcome; total sleep was 433 minutes with the filter and 449 with clear lenses. [Bigalke et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleh.2021.02.004) A contrasting insomnia pilot improved ratings and actigraphic total sleep after one week of amber lenses, but included only 14 adults. [Shechter et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2017.10.015) That signal is too small to establish who benefits.

### Conclusion

Evening blue-enriched light can delay circadian timing and, when sufficiently long or bright, sleep onset. Whole-night effects under more ordinary conditions appear small and inconsistent. Managing brightness, duration, proximity to bedtime, and screen use in bed is therefore more defensible than treating wavelength as the only cause. Larger preregistered trials must determine whether filters help unusually sensitive or sleep-disordered groups.
