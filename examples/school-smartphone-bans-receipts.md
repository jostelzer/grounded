> Restrictive English schools had lower school-time phone use than permissive schools, with an adjusted difference of −0.67 hours daily; this was an observational association.

- **Goodyear et al. 2025** · full text · supported — “Adolescents attending schools with restrictive, compared to permissive policies had lower phone (adjusted mean difference −0.67 h, 95% CI −0.92 to −0.43, p = 0.00024) and social media time (adjusted mean difference −0.54 h, 95% CI −0.74 to −0.36, p = 0.00018) during school time” … “We conducted a cross-sectional observational study with adolescents from 30 English secondary schools”

## C016 · paragraph 4, item 2, sentence 1

> Academic engagement showed no detectable advantage in a preregistered South Australian comparison after one month.

- **King et al. 2024** · full text · supported — “However, there was no significant between-group difference in academic engagement at follow-up, d = 0.03, 95% CI [−0.14, 0.20].” … “The aim of this preregistered study was to evaluate the South Australian mobile phone ban's effects on students' problematic phone use, academic engagement, school belonging, and bullying.” … “a 2 (phone ban: yes/no) × 2 (time: baseline, 1-month follow-up) repeated-measures design was employed.”

## C019 · paragraph 5, item 1, sentence 1

> Lower psychological distress was associated with bans in a secondary South Australian analysis; its abstract describes the difference as small.

- **Baggio et al. 2025** · abstract · supported — “We conducted a secondary analysis of a natural experiment using an emulated trial framework to examine the effects of a mobile phone ban implemented in Australian schools in 2023” … “In this emulated trial of Australian secondary school students, the implementation of a mobile phone ban was associated with small but significant reductions in psychological distress and negative affect.”

## C020 · paragraph 5, item 2, sentence 1

> English schools with restrictive policies showed no evidence of better mental wellbeing.

- **Goodyear et al. 2025** · full text · supported — “Mean WEMWBS score was 47 (SD = 9) with no evidence of a difference between groups (adjusted mean difference −0.48, 95% CI −2.05 to 1.06, p = 0.62).” (bridge: WEMWBS is the mental wellbeing score; no group difference means no detected wellbeing advantage.)
