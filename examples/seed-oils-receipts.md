## Whole-review interpretation

**preserved** — Culinary seed oils are heterogeneous exposures. Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated oils reliably lowers short-term LDL, while coronary-event benefits depend on the replacement regimen and consistent mortality benefit is not established. ## Outcome certainty (separate from source support)

- Oil composition and applicability: moderate — Analytical and scoping evidence; food-level synthesis quality limited. Named oils and variants differ substantially. A compositional comparison does not establish a clinical effect.
- Short-term lipid response to specified replacement: moderate — Short feeding trials and indirect network contrasts; several trials have commercial ties. Direction is more consistent versus saturated fats than versus another unsaturated oil. Lipid markers do not measure clinical events; high doses and selected adults limit extrapolation.
- Coronary events after PUFA substitution: low — Old trials have co-interventions, incomplete recovered data and variable adherence. Broad and restricted trial syntheses differ; many reuse the same trials. Historical mixed-fat interventions do not isolate modern bottled seed oils.
- All-cause mortality after total PUFA increase: moderate — Cochrane review includes many trials above low summary risk of bias. Overall estimate is near null despite heterogeneous regimens. Total PUFA includes supplements and mixed omega families.
- Causal interpretation of prospective cardiovascular biomarkers: low — Residual confounding and genetic pleiotropy remain material. Favorable and null estimates vary by endpoint and biomarker. Circulating concentration is not randomized dietary oil exposure.
- Prospective linoleic-acid biomarkers and diabetes: low — Observational biomarkers reflect metabolism as well as intake. Individual omega-6 biomarkers differ, though LA associations are generally inverse. Association cannot establish a prevention effect from eating oil.
- Randomized omega-6 and diabetes diagnosis: very_low — Sparse omega-6-specific trials inherit trial-quality limitations. Too little direct diagnosis evidence to establish consistency. Mixed PUFA and glucose markers are not isolated omega-6 diagnosis effects.
- Common circulating inflammatory markers: moderate — Small heterogeneous feeding trials; review overlap is not resolved. Most measured markers do not increase, but markers and comparators differ. Circulating markers do not capture every tissue pathway or ex vivo response.
- Mechanistic interpretation and dietary ratios: low — Narrative mechanisms and selected studies are vulnerable to selection. Ratio and pathway interpretations conflict. Molecular plausibility cannot estimate ordinary dietary disease risk.
- EPA and stimulated oxylipin response: moderate — Blinding and dietary control strengthen design, but 80 randomized and 52 completers create attrition concerns. One recent experiment needs independent replication. Healthy adults, oleic-acid substitution, twelve weeks and ex vivo challenge limit clinical inference.
- Cooking oils and mortality or prevalent cardiovascular disease: low — Questionnaires, residual confounding and cross-sectional reverse causation. US prospective and Chinese cross-sectional associations differ. Named oils, populations, diets and disease definitions differ.
- Oxidation under controlled heating and storage: moderate — Controlled assays support chemistry; selected oils and laboratory protocols. Temperature and oil identity change amounts and compounds. Chemistry endpoint is direct; extrapolation to home cooking is limited.
- Human disease from repeated oil heating: very_low — Evidence dominated by narrative toxicology and experimental animal studies. Comparable direct human disease studies are lacking. Animal feeding and aldehyde assays do not establish ordinary human risk.
- Cancer outcomes from omega-6 or total PUFA: low — Mixed observational and trial evidence with variable bias. Cancer sites and PUFA definitions differ. Total PUFA and very high doses do not isolate usual seed-oil exposure.
- Cognition and inflammatory bowel disease: very_low — Trial reviews systematic but direct omega-6 evidence sparse. Sparse comparable omega-6 trials cannot establish consistency. Most evidence concerns other PUFA families.
