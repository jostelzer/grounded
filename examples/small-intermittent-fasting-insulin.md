## Does intermittent fasting improve insulin sensitivity?

**TL;DR** — Sometimes, but the average benefit is small and usually appears alongside lower energy intake or weight loss. Intermittent fasting outperforms eating without restriction on some fasting insulin measures, yet it is generally no better than ordinary calorie restriction; a weight-independent benefit from eating early remains plausible but unproven.

### Benefits are small against no diet and mostly vanish against matched restriction

- In adults with overweight or obesity, fasting reduced fasting insulin versus non-intervention by an [SMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardized_mean_difference) of −0.21 (95% [CI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_interval) −0.40 to −0.02). Yet across 99 trials (n=6,582), fasting variants and continuous restriction produced only trivial changes in [HOMA-IR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostatic_model_assessment) and fasting glucose versus ad-libitum eating, with no [HbA1c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycated_hemoglobin) advantage [Sun et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102519), [Semnani-Azad et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-082007).
- A 20-trial meta-analysis restricted to same-calorie comparisons found some fasting-insulin and HOMA-IR advantages in sparse long-term subgroups, but concluded that intermittent fasting was not superior overall to continuous restriction [Hamsho et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.numecd.2024.103805).

### The regimens are not metabolically interchangeable

| Regimen | Best controlled evidence | Interpretation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-restricted eating (daily 6–10 h window) | An early 6-h, weight-stable crossover improved insulin sensitivity in 8 men; a 12-week same-calorie trial (n=41), a 3-week direct-measurement trial (n=14), and a 12-week pilot (n=20) were null for insulin sensitivity or glycaemic indices | Possible timing effect, not replicated consistently | [Sutton et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2018.04.010), [Maruthur et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.7326/m23-3132), [Andriessen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-022-05752-z), [Bantle et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/oby.23620) |
| Alternate-day or 5:2 fasting | One-year trials found alternate-day fasting no better than daily restriction for fasting insulin/HOMA-IR (n=100), and 5:2 equivalent for HbA1c in type 2 diabetes (n=137) | Useful restriction pattern, not a distinct sensitivity treatment | [Trepanowski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.0936), [Carter et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.0756) |
| Periodic multi-day fasting | Exploratory analyses of three fasting-mimicking-diet cycles suggested lower insulin resistance; in six lean men, 60 h of water fasting instead reduced insulin-mediated glucose uptake by 46% | Sparse, protocol-specific, and acutely bidirectional | [Brandhorst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45260-9), [van der Crabben et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1210/jc.2006-2491) |

### Early timing is promising, but outcome choice changes the answer

- Early intermittent fasting improved mixed-meal glucose exposure more than daily restriction in 209 adults at diabetes risk at 6 months, but the difference disappeared by 18 months; that is glucose tolerance, not direct insulin sensitivity [Teong et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02287-7).
- In type 2 diabetes, 10-h eating lowered 24-h glucose (6.8 versus 7.6 mmol/L) while a [hyperinsulinaemic–euglycaemic clamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_clamp_technique) showed no hepatic or peripheral sensitivity improvement. Fasting glucose, HOMA-IR, HbA1c, and clamp sensitivity therefore should not be treated as synonyms [Andriessen et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-022-05752-z).
- Six-month time-restricted eating and calorie restriction lowered HbA1c similarly (−0.91% and −0.94%) in type 2 diabetes; supervised fasting on three nonconsecutive days per week also lowered HbA1c and insulin dose in insulin-treated disease. Both findings may reflect weight loss, energy reduction, or medication changes rather than an intrinsic sensitivity effect [Pavlou et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.39337), [Obermayer et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc22-1622).

### Short trials look tolerable; long-term adherence and higher-risk use need care

- Across 15 trials (n=1,365), intermittent fasting did not increase fatigue, headache, or dropout, but estimates were too imprecise to establish long-term or regimen-specific safety [Zhong et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12937-024-00975-9).
- Alternate-day fasting had 38% dropout versus 29% with daily restriction at one year, and adherence declined in several year-long trials. No severe hypoglycaemia occurred in the small insulin-treated trial, but it used counselling, glucose monitoring, and medication adjustment—conditions that limit generalisation to unsupervised fasting [Trepanowski et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2017.0936), [Semnani-Azad et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-082007), [Obermayer et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc22-1622).
