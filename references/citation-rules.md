# Citation rules

Fabricated, misattributed, or retracted citations are the failure mode that destroys trust in AI-written reviews. These rules make that failure structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged.

## The ledger is the only source of citations

- `sources.json` holds every candidate source with its DOI, metadata, abstract, the query that found it, and a verification record.
- A paper is citable only if it is in the ledger, its peer-review status is appropriate for the field, and `verify_citations.py` has marked it `verified` after checking both its Crossref bibliographic record and Crossref's publisher/Retraction Watch update metadata for integrity signals (retraction, withdrawal, removal, expression of concern). Crossref type and index inclusion do not independently prove peer review.
- If you remember a paper that belongs in the review, find it with `find_papers.py` (search its title or distinctive phrases). If the index cannot find it, or the DOI does not verify, it is not cited. No exceptions for famous papers.
- `format_references.py` builds the reference list from the Crossref metadata stored at verification. You never type an author list, journal, volume, or page by hand.

## What may be cited

| Source type | Status |
|---|---|
| Peer-reviewed journal article (Crossref type `journal-article`) | Yes |
| Peer-reviewed conference paper (`proceedings-article`) | Yes in fields where conferences are the venue (computing, some engineering). Verifier warns; override knowingly. |
| Book chapter, monograph (`book-chapter`, `book`) | For definitions, theory, or history only; not as evidence of an empirical effect |
| Preprint (`posted-content`, bioRxiv, medRxiv, arXiv, SSRN) | Not as evidence. May be mentioned once for a very recent development, labelled "(preprint, not peer reviewed)", never load-bearing. Requires `--include-preprints` on search and `--allow-preprints` on verify — a deliberate act. |
| Retracted paper | Only to state that it was retracted and what that means for the literature |
| **Clinical guideline / consensus statement published as a journal article** (e.g. an AAP policy statement in *Pediatrics*, a society guideline in *Circulation*) — has a DOI, Crossref type `journal-article` | **Citable**, and often should be. Cite it as *guidance*, not as evidence: "the AAP recommends X", never "studies show X". It carries the weight of the committee's reading of the evidence, not of a new study. |
| Standalone guideline, agency report, or white paper with no journal publication (PDF on an organisation's site, possibly with a DOI) | Mention in prose as context; no entry in the sources block. If the recommendation matters, cite the guideline's own underlying primary studies. |
| **Tertiary reference works — StatPearls, UpToDate, Merck Manual, textbook chapters, encyclopedias, Wikipedia** | **Never citable, for anything.** These summarise other people's work and are frequently out of date. StatPearls is the trap: it is indexed in PubMed and will appear in search results looking like a peer-reviewed article. It is an NCBI Bookshelf entry, not a study. If it says something you want to use, find and cite the primary source it drew on. |
| Websites, news, blogs, datasets, preprint servers as venues | Not citable in the review body. |
| Paper with no DOI | Not citable unless a DOI can be found; very old papers without DOIs can be cited with a PMID only if the user accepts that and the PubMed record is confirmed |

## Keys and in-text form

- Keys are generated by the search script: `FirstAuthorYearFirstTitleWord` (e.g. `Kuyken2022effectiveness`), made unique with a letter suffix if needed.
- In the draft, cite as `claim [@Key].` or `claim [@Key1; @Key2].` Place the citation immediately after the words it supports and before sentence-ending punctuation; in explicit bullet mode, this will usually be the end of the supported bullet. Never write `claim. [@Key]` and never begin a sentence with a citation key.
- **Default rendered form is an author–year DOI link** (`--style bracket`, the style flag name is historical): the reader sees `Carter 2018` as a plain clickable link in `claim [Carter 2018](DOI).` Square brackets appear only as markdown link syntax and must never survive into the visible text — no bare `[Carter 2018]`, no `[1]`, no `(Carter, 2018)`, and no `claim. [Carter 2018](DOI)`. One author `Carter 2018`, two `Carter & Clifton 2018`, three or more `Carter et al. 2018`; several sources for one point are comma-separated links. Same author and year twice → `2018a` / `2018b`.
- **Journal PDF/HTML exception:** keep that author–year markdown as the canonical review, then let `export_review.py` replace each DOI link in the journal artifact with a DOI-linked superscript number. The source order is `claim [Author](DOI).`; the journal render deliberately becomes `claim.` followed by the raised number. Numbers are assigned by first appearance and the journal References section is reordered to match. The superscript closes against the preceding supported sentence, clause, or quotation, after its punctuation. It must never be the grammatical opening of a sentence, paragraph, bullet, or caption; the exporter rejects that placement. A DOI-only source cell in a comparison table is allowed because it labels the row rather than opening a sentence. This exception never changes the chat or source markdown.
- Vancouver, APA and Nature remain available on request via `--style`.
- A source cited several times keeps the same tag.

## Citing accurately

- **Guidance vs evidence.** When citing a guideline, the sentence must make clear it is a recommendation ("NICE advises…", "the 2024 AAP statement recommends…"). Never let a guideline stand in for the trial evidence behind it; if the evidence matters, cite the trials.
- **Cite the study that did the work.** If a review reports a finding from a trial and the finding matters, cite the trial (find it) — and optionally the review too.
- **Cite what the source actually says.** Check the abstract or full text for the specific claim; do not cite a paper for a point it mentions only in its introduction.
- **Secondary citation** ("as reported in [2]") is acceptable only when the primary source is inaccessible; say so.
- **Date-sensitive claims**: when citing a review for the state of the field, note its search date if a later major study changes the picture.
- **Multiple citations**: list the strongest first (the large trial, the most recent meta-analysis); avoid citing eight papers for one sentence when two carry the point.
- **Self-consistency**: the same finding should carry the same citation everywhere it appears.

## Verification workflow

1. `python3 scripts/verify_citations.py --ledger sources.json` after searching, before drafting — so the ledger contains only verified candidates.
2. `python3 scripts/verify_citations.py --ledger sources.json --cited review_draft.md` after drafting — confirms every key in the draft exists and is verified.
3. `python3 scripts/format_references.py ...` — refuses if anything is unknown or unverified.

If a source fails verification: check the DOI for typos; search the title again to get the correct DOI; if it cannot be verified, remove the citation and, if the claim depended on it, the claim.

The ledger keeps two distinct fields under `verification`: `bibliographic_status` and `retraction_status`. Both come from the same authoritative Crossref work record. `retraction_status` is `clear`, `flagged`, or `not_checked`; it is `flagged` for retractions, withdrawals, removals, and expressions of concern, and a citation is citable only when the bibliographic status is `verified` and retraction status is `clear`. The saved `retraction_signals` identify the severity, the Crossref relation (`updated-by`, `update-to`, or a defensive notice-title match), linked DOI, and metadata source. Corrigenda and errata never flag the entry; they are saved separately as `correction_notices` and rendered as a linked "Correction:" note on the reference entry.

## Styles

| Style | In text | Reference list |
|---|---|---|
| `bracket` (default) | `Carter et al. 2018` linked to the DOI | Alphabetical, one line each: **All Authors (Year)** Title. *Journal*. DOI link — every author named, never "et al." |
| `vancouver` | `[1]`, `[2,3]`, `[4–6]` in order of appearance | Numbered; all authors; title; journal; year;volume(issue):pages; DOI |
| `apa` | `(Author et al., 2022)` | Alphabetical; APA 7th form; DOI as URL |
| `nature` | `^1,2^` superscripts | Numbered; all authors; journal in italics, volume bold |

Journals often require a specific style; ask if the review is for submission.
The canonical GROUNDED journal PDF is not produced with `--style nature`; its renderer performs the journal-only linked numbering described above while preserving the validated chat markdown.

## What to tell the reader

Reviews carry no methods section, so the sources block with its DOIs is the audit trail — every line is resolvable. Say nothing when verification passed. Bibliographic failures, unavailable Crossref checks, and integrity flags are fixed or removed, not published with warning symbols; the only annotation a reference entry may carry is the factual "Correction:" note for a recorded corrigendum or erratum.
