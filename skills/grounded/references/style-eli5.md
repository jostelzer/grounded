# ELI5 style

Use only when requested. Write a connected explanation for a reader with no science background, with ordinary words, patient pacing and the reporting voice in `writing-guide.md`. Simpler language must preserve the evidence's meaning and uncertainty. Use flowing paragraphs; if the user also requests bullets, validate as `bullets` and apply this plain-language register.

## Build understanding

Open with the question in everyday words and a citation-free **TL;DR** of 1–3 sentences. Start the explanation from a familiar fact or a clearly described observation, then introduce ideas in the order needed to understand the answer. An invented anecdote is not a substitute for context.

Each section develops a useful question or point. Headings can be plain statements or natural questions; the body answers them with connected paragraphs. Explain necessary ideas before relying on them. If a later limitation changes an earlier finding, qualify that finding from the start rather than saving the limitation for a surprise.

Describe a decisive study's comparison in ordinary terms when this makes its result understandable. Related studies can contribute within the same paragraph; one study per paragraph and one new fact per sentence are not rules. Short sentences often help, but adjacent sentences must still explain how the ideas connect.

An analogy is optional. Use it only when it makes a difficult relationship easier to understand without adding an unsupported claim. Explain its limit when material; neither repeated callbacks nor an explicit retirement is required. A direct explanation can be sufficient.

Finish with a plain answer and its remaining uncertainty, with no new evidence or instructions for the reader or researchers. Keep the shared scope/methods, Sources and receipts. Length follows the selected size in `sizes.md`.

## Language and quantities

Use everyday words and explain unfamiliar ones at first use. Replace technical shorthand with its meaning: “people in the study” instead of “participants” when this reads naturally; describe what was compared instead of relying on a trial-design label. Ordinary words such as “study,” “measurement” and “finding” are acceptable when their meaning is clear. Warmth comes from clarity and pacing, without required “we,” “you,” question-and-answer exchanges or spoken-language tricks.

Select quantities that help explain the finding. Use understandable units, frequencies or appropriate rounded values, preserving the comparator and denominator. Render uncertainty as a plain-language range when possible. A number limit must never strip away the range or comparison needed to interpret a result; supporting detail may move to a table. Do not invent familiar-size comparisons or turn an imprecise finding into “no effect.”

Rewrite jargon when possible. For an unavoidable specialist term, explain it simply and give its verified first-use link under `writing-guide.md`. Keep empirical citations beside the claims they support, before terminal punctuation. Do not collect them into a source dump or remove them to simplify the appearance.

Figure captions also use connected everyday sentences. Describe the parts, lines or symbols that need explanation and preserve the uncertainty. Follow `figure-captions.md`; do not switch to structured bullet captions unless bullets were requested. Apply the shared whole-article editorial check to the explanation and captions together.
