# Popular-science style

Use for the default review or an explicit popular-science, magazine or science-journalism request. Write a feature a curious adult wants to keep reading. **A sustained story is required.** The reader should encounter something worth explaining, follow the work of understanding it and finish with a changed view of the opening subject. Source accuracy and narrative pleasure are joint requirements. Apply the shared reporting voice in `writing-guide.md`.

## Find the story before composing

While researching, collect the concrete material a story needs: a revealing observation, an experimental action, a consequential comparison, or a documented change in understanding. Ask what was puzzling, what researchers tried, what the result made possible to explain, and what question remained. Read relevant methods and earlier work for those details; an abstract's conclusion is seldom enough. Record source-backed details in the existing notes and synthesis.

In **Throughline**, describe the reader's journey in a short paragraph: the opening subject and question, the explanation that develops, the evidence that moves it forward, and the understanding reached at the end. The subject may be a phenomenon, object, place, idea or investigation; a human protagonist is optional. A list of topics, a catchy first paragraph followed by study summaries, or repeated reminders to be cautious does not supply a story.

Choose an angle that makes the subject worth following: what will the reader understand differently? If several angles could work, briefly consider alternatives against the available evidence before committing. Build movement through questions the explanation actually raises and answers, without withholding essential context for suspense. A related study earns space when it develops that inquiry; relevance to the broad topic alone is insufficient. Preserve the requested scope and material contrary findings when selecting or cutting material.

Choose a form suited to the evidence. A discovery story follows attempts and consequences; an explanatory mystery follows observations and competing explanations; a journey through a system follows a process across places or scales. These are possibilities, not section templates. Historical development can be compelling when the sources establish it. Do not invent a chronology, rivalry, surprise, motive or scene to manufacture drama. Without documented personal history, tell the story of the phenomenon or the experiment itself.

## Shape of the feature

- **Headline:** recast the research question honestly. Avoid promises of certainty or a payoff the evidence cannot deliver. Establish equivalence before switching between a brand and generic name or other unfamiliar synonyms.
- **Standfirst:** 1–3 italic, citation-free sentences that make the subject and its interest clear. Offer the article's promise without packing the entire evidence assessment into a miniature abstract.
- **Opening:** enter through a specific, sourced observation or action that creates a real question. Give enough orientation early for the reader to know why this question is worth following. A generic everyday vignette, rhetorical question or surprising adjective alone is not a hook. The opening subject must continue to matter after the introduction.
- **Body:** weave explanation into the developing story. Give background when an observation or action creates a need for it, then return to what that knowledge explains. Let the result of one section motivate the next question. Crossheads orient and invite; they must not partition the piece into disconnected mini-reviews. Include material contrary findings where they change the explanation.
- **Ending:** deliver the understanding promised at the start. Show what can now be explained about that subject and where a consequential question remains. End on a concrete implication, observation or unresolved puzzle when appropriate. A summary list, generalized caution or automatic “more research is needed” does not provide the payoff.
- **Apparatus:** retain the shared scope/methods disclosure, generated Sources and receipts. Word budgets and research depth follow the selected size.

## Explain through concrete evidence

Develop the phenomenon before naming an unfamiliar mechanism. Give a tangible example of what a concept means before asking the reader to reason with it. Gloss necessary terms at first use; a hyperlink never substitutes for explanation in the article. Explain a method through the action and comparison it enables. Omit procedure that does not help the story, but retain the detail that lets the reader understand how a conclusion was reached.

A study can anchor several paragraphs, with other evidence entering to extend or challenge a specific point. Introduce researchers when their actions or identity explain the investigation, rather than restarting every finding with an author name. There is no actor quota. Surnames from citation tags and accurate generic descriptions are safe; given names and institutions must be copied from the ledger's structured author records through the synthesis `actors` field. Never infer affiliations, expand initials or invent scenes, participants, motives or sensory details.

Choose the numbers that carry the comparison and explain their scale. Prefer absolute effects or frequencies when available and appropriate; never derive an absolute risk from a relative effect without the necessary baseline. Use parentheses or a table for supporting precision, preserving units, denominators and uncertainty. Numeric density follows what the reader needs to understand, not a fixed count per sentence or section. Do not obscure a material difference merely to sound conversational.

Use concrete subjects and active, familiar verbs. Alternate close description with wider explanation as the subject needs; a succession of abstract verdicts makes reading laborious. Let sentence length vary naturally. Questions, analogies and quotations may add clarity or rhythm, but do not use them to decorate thin explanation. An analogy must preserve the relationship it explains and stop where it becomes misleading. Warmth, vividness and an accessible voice are compatible with objective reporting.

Give the useful finding before discussing its limitations, unless a limitation is needed to prevent an immediate misunderstanding. Integrate material uncertainty into the explanation: what remains unknown, why that matters here, or how it led to another experiment. Do not append a methodological moral to every paragraph. Attribution can live in a local citation; repeatedly beginning with “the review reports” or “clinical accounts suggest” moves attention from the subject to the paperwork. Name the source in prose when its identity or judgment matters.

Quote a source only when its wording contributes something the paraphrase would lose. Use short, verbatim-checkable excerpts from stored text, retain qualifications, attribute them and cite the claim. Written words must never be presented as an interview or remembered speech. All empirical details, including experiences, retain the shared citation and source-support requirements.

## Make the pictures part of the explanation

Use figures at the point where the story asks the reader to imagine a relationship: a hidden structure, a sequence, competing arrangements, an experimental contrast or a change of scale. Plan what becomes understandable from seeing it. An attractive portrait or object with a reassuring slogan does not count as an explanatory figure. Apply the whole-figure deletion test in `figure-generation-contract.md`; captions under `figure-captions.md` explain the visible relationship and its scope rather than supplying a message absent from the image.

## Read it as an article

Complete the shared editorial read with the ledger and production notes out of view. Describe what draws the reader in, how the question develops and what the ending delivers, pointing to actual passages. Locate any point where reading becomes work because of abstract phrasing, unfamiliar terms, repeated caveats or an unexplained jump. Revise the passage rather than awarding a style score. If the piece could be rearranged into largely independent sections without changing the journey, rebuild its structure. A source-supported but dull or fragmented feature is not ready.

The craft rationale and published examples are in `popsci-craft-notes.md`, for consultation when learning or revising this style. Borrow techniques, not distinctive phrasing, subjects or a publication's complete formula.
